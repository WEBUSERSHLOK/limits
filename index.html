Calc Topics:

What is a Limit? The Intended Height

A limit is the intended height that $f(x)$ approaches as $x$ gets arbitrarily close to $c$, regardless of whether the point $f(c)$ is defined!

1. Left & Right Disagree: $\lim_{x \to c^-} \ne \lim_{x \to c^+}$ (Jump)
2. Unbounded: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{x^2} = \infty$ (Vertical Asymptote)
3. Oscillating: $\lim_{x \to 0} \sin(1/x)$ (Never settles)

Rule #1: Direct Substitution First

Always "plug and chug" first! For polynomials, radicals, and trig in their domain, substitution always gives the exact limit.

0/0 Indeterminate Forms

When you get $\frac{0}{0}$: 1) Factor & Cancel, 2) Rationalize with Conjugates, or 3) Multiply through by LCD for complex fractions.

The 3-Part Continuity Checklist

Continuous at $x=c$ iff: 1. $f(c)$ exists, 2. $\lim_{x \to c} f(x)$ exists, and 3. $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = f(c)$.

Infinite Limits & Vertical Asymptotes

When direct substitution yields $\frac{\text{nonzero}}{0}$, test $x = c \pm 0.01$ to identify whether the curve goes to $+\infty$ or $-\infty$.

Limits at Infinity & Horizontal Asymptotes

Compare degrees: Bottom heavy $\to 0$, Equal degrees $\to$ ratio of leading coefficients, Top heavy $\to \text{DNE} (\pm\infty)$.

The Squeeze Theorem & Trig Limits

If $g(x) \le f(x) \le h(x)$ and $\lim g = \lim h = L$, then $\lim f = L$. Memorize: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1$ and $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x} = 0$.

The Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT)

If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$, it must take on every value between $f(a)$ and $f(b)$ at least once.

Kinematic Equations of Motion ($a = \text{const}$)

Use the 4 constant-acceleration equations. Assume $g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ downward for free-fall.

$$v = v_0 + at$$
$$\Delta x = v_0 t + \frac{1}{2}at^2$$
$$v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\Delta x$$
$$\Delta x = \left(\frac{v_0 + v}{2}\right)t$$
